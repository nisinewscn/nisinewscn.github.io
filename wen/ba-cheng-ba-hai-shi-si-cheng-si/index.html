八成八，还是四成四

88.2 Per Cent, or 43.6?

逆流 · 2026年8月17日

这几年，团结政府最常被拿出来说的一句话是：吵归吵，好歹没散，这就是稳。

八月十六日，行动党特别代表大会表决续留联邦政府。一千八百五十七票赞成，两百四十二票反对，多数报道都用的数字，是88.2巴仙。

这是发出选票的比例，不是有效票的比例。

选票发出两千一百零六张，有效票两千零九十九张，七张作废，出席大会的代表两千两百二十三人，出席率约52.1巴仙。全国登记代表呢？一千七百零四个符合资格的支部，四千二百六十四人。这个数字，出现在东方日报的报道里，不在大部分标题里。

一千八百五十七除以四千二百六十四，43.6巴仙，连四成四都不到。

没人问。

同一天，还有两句话。首相安华在公正党大会上说："我选择稳定，也选择透过经济增长为人民带来繁荣；为了让这两个原则都能站得住，我选择继续在联邦层面与国阵、巫统合作。"三天前，公正党青年团团长慕哈末卡米尔在马六甲说，团结政府的稳定性正受到"两面派"人士考验，"这种政治背叛行为阻碍政府对改革议程的努力"。他没有点名是谁。

如果这份稳，本身就稳，一场加开的党大会，不必首相亲自出面喊话，也不必青年团团长隔空点名提防"两面派"。换个算法，这份稳，看起来更像是被撑住的，不是自己站着的。

当然，也有另一种可能。这是一场加开的特大会，不是每年例行的党代表大会，两千多个支部代表未必人人排得出档期，出席率52巴仙算不算低，行动党自己过去大会的记录里没有一份报道拿来对照过。这一点，本文不猜。能确定的，只是标题选择讲出哪一个数字，把另一个数字，留给读者自己去找。

这件事，不必猜，也不必等。

能做的事很具体。行动党中央委员会这次已经公开说过，续留还是退出的决定不由中委会拍板，完全交给大会代表。往后每一次重大表决，可以要求新闻稿模板把"登记代表总数"跟"有效票比例"放进同一段。成本几乎是零，多打一行字而已。

头三十天能做的更具体：把这次八月十六日大会的登记代表总数，用一份补充文告的形式，公开写进这次的结果里，不必等到下一次表决才开始。改变的是，下一次开票，读者不用自己拼东方日报、星报、Sinar Harian三份报道，才能凑出这两个数字。

下次再听到"团结政府很稳"，不妨先问一句：这份稳，分母是多少？

88.2 Per Cent, or 43.6?

In recent years, the line most often used to defend the unity government has been this: it argues, but at least it has not fallen apart, and that counts as stable.

On 16 August, DAP held a special national congress to vote on remaining in the federal government. One thousand eight hundred and fifty-seven votes in favour, two hundred and forty-two against, and most reports led with one figure: 88.2 per cent.

That is a share of the ballots issued, not the valid votes.

2,106 ballots were issued, 2,099 valid votes were counted, seven were spoiled, and 2,223 delegates attended, an attendance rate of about 52.1 per cent. How many delegates are registered nationwide? 4,264, from 1,704 qualified branches. That number appeared in Oriental Daily's report, not in most of the headlines.

Divide 1,857 by 4,264: 43.6 per cent, not even 44.

Nobody asked.

That same day, two other lines were spoken. PM Anwar Ibrahim told the PKR National Congress: "I choose stability. I choose the prosperity of the people through economic growth. And I choose to continue working with Barisan Nasional and Umno at the federal level, so that these two principles can be upheld." Three days earlier, in Malacca, PKR Youth chief Mohamad Kamil said the unity government's stability was being tested by "two-faced" figures, whose "political betrayal hinders the government's reform efforts." He did not name anyone.

If this stability were stable on its own terms, an extraordinary party congress would not need the prime minister personally making the case, nor a youth chief warning at a distance about the "two-faced". Counted a different way, this stability looks propped up rather than standing on its own.

There is, of course, another reading. This was an extraordinary congress, not the party's regular annual gathering, and not every delegate from over two thousand branches could necessarily clear their schedule. Whether a 52 per cent turnout counts as low, no report has set it against DAP's own past congress records. This piece will not guess at that. What can be said for certain is which figure the headline chose to state, leaving the other one for readers to find themselves.

There is no need to guess at this, or to wait.

What could actually be done here is specific. DAP's Central Executive Committee has already said, on the record, that the stay-or-leave decision would not be made by the CEC itself, but handed entirely to congress delegates. For every major vote from now on, it could require the press release template to put the total number of registered delegates in the same paragraph as the valid-vote percentage. The cost is close to nothing, one extra line of text.

What could be done within the first thirty days is more specific still: issue a supplementary statement adding the 16 August congress's own registered-delegate total to the published result, rather than waiting for the next vote. What would change: at the next count, readers would not need to piece together three separate reports, from Oriental Daily, The Star and Sinar Harian, just to work out both figures.

Next time you hear that the unity government is stable, it is worth asking one thing first: what is the denominator behind that stability?
