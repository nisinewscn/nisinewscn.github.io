谁看过纳吉那页附件

Who Has Seen Najib's Page of Conditions?

逆流 · 2026年9月19日

九月十八日，首相署法律事务组一纸文告：国家元首御准纳吉有条件特赦，居家服刑至二〇二八年八月二十三日，条件是缴清五千万令吉罚款；违反任何一项条件，特赦即失效，立刻回监狱。文告也写明，这是联邦直辖区特赦局第六十四次会议的决定，涉及的是SRC案的刑罚。

文告到此。接下来的一天半，比文告长得多。

特赦不等于翻案。SRC案的定罪还在，1MDB案的上诉还在，反贪会主席阿都哈林十九日说得直白：仍在审理中的案件维持现状。

然后风就起了，而且是从政府里面起的。

安华周五祈祷后见记者：特赦不影响政府打贪的决心，所有贪污案件按法律程序继续。他说，身为首相，他的责任是就法律影响、国家稳定和法治原则提供建议，但宪法把绝对权力交给了国家元首，“我希望大家接受”。至于居家服刑的标准作业程序，他说那属于内政部的权限。

当晚，希盟主席理事会发文告，支持首相的立场，也支持总检察长和联邦直辖区部长向特赦局提出的意见，然后加了一句：“没有任何人可以凌驾于法律之上。”

十九日上午，巫统宣传主任、同时是首相署法律及体制改革部长的阿莎丽娜回了一句：特赦权写在联邦宪法第四十二条，把行使宪赋特赦权形容为“凌驾于法律之上”，不恰当。她的收尾跟希盟几乎一字不差：“没有人可以凌驾于法律之上，也没有人可以凌驾于宪法之上。”

同一句话，两边说，两个方向。

马华总会长魏家祥十八日就说过：司法程序和特赦程序是两回事，各有宪法依据。

下午，行动党中委会开了约三小时的会。秘书长陆兆福出来宣布：他将辞去交通部长，作为党对这件事的回应；其他行动党正副部长留任，“以确保政治稳定”。他也重申，行动党尊重国家元首的特赦权。

公正党总秘书傅芝雅同日表态：与希盟主席理事会立场一致。阿末扎希同日说，团结政府各政党的关系仍然良好。

于是到了周六傍晚，团结政府对同一个决定的回应是这样的：首相说希望大家接受；希盟说无人凌驾法律；巫统说特赦权本来就写在宪法里，马华说的也是这个意思；行动党一人将辞官，其余人留下。

四种回应，一个内阁。

伊斯兰党总秘书达基尤丁十八日下午的文告，星洲日报转述如下：感谢国家元首，这项发展对纳吉的家人意义重大，祈求他在家中服刑期间继续健康，相信他仍能凭其理念、观点及经验为国家利益作出贡献。四句话，说完就完。

巫统忙的是另一件事。十八日当天，巫统发起筹款，帮纳吉缴那五千万。东方日报十九日早上的数字：突破一百万令吉，还欠四千九百万。

十九日早上，印有监狱局标志的车辆开进了他在大使路的住家。内政部长赛夫丁在居林说，监狱局还在等特赦局的正式通知函，通常由首相署法律事务组发出，收到才知道下一步。他也坦言，我国目前没有居家服刑的专门法律，内政部此前已获内阁原则上同意，着手起草居家服刑法案，为的是纾解还押候审者造成的监狱拥挤。

所以周六晚上，纳吉还在加影。

“没有人凌驾法律”，希盟说了，巫统也说了。捍卫自由律师团的拉尼雅翻的是同一部宪法的第四十二条第四款，讲的却是另一件事。

按她的读法，这一款的机制是这样的：国家元首行使特赦权，是在联邦直辖区特赦局的建议之下；特赦局里坐着代表内阁的联邦直辖区部长，坐着作为政府法律顾问的总检察长，其余成员也是按政府的建议委任。所以安华说“绝对权力”，她说“在特赦局的建议下行使”，两个人描述的是同一条文的两端：决定权在元首，建议权在一张政府坐了大半的桌子上。她因此认为，政府不能说这件事与自己无关。

第四十二条一直都在，特赦局的座位也一直都在。这一天半里新的，是同一个政府对同一个决定给出的回应，有四种。

家里的账本被人翻开，一家人各说各的。外人不用插嘴，只要把每个人的话抄下来，放在一起。

倒是有一件事，所有人都答得上来，却没有人做：那份特赦条件到底写了什么。拉菲兹要求全部公开，独立新闻中心要求公开法律依据、完整条件和监察方式。文告上只有一句：须遵守所规定的条件。

这件事不需要修宪。特赦局的通知函一发出，首相署法律事务组把附带条件原文贴出来；安华自己说过执行程序属内政部权限，内政部就把程序一并说明。不花一分钱。三十天内做不做，一看就知道。

一个决定，一份文告，四种回应。解释已经够多了。少的是那一页附件。

Who Has Seen Najib's Page of Conditions?

On 18 September the Legal Affairs Division of the Prime Minister's Department issued one statement: the King (Yang di-Pertuan Agong) had consented to a conditional pardon for Najib Razak, allowing him to serve the remainder of his sentence under house arrest until 23 August 2028, on condition that he pays the RM50 million fine in full. Breach any condition and the pardon lapses; he goes straight back to prison. The statement also recorded that this was the decision of the 64th meeting of the Federal Territories Pardons Board, and that it concerned the sentence in the SRC International case.

The statement ends there. The day and a half that followed ran far longer than it did.

A pardon is not an acquittal. The SRC conviction stands; the 1MDB appeal is still running. The chairman of the anti-corruption commission (MACC), Abdul Halim, put it plainly on the 19th: cases still before the courts stay exactly as they are.

Then the wind got up, and it got up inside the government.

Anwar Ibrahim, speaking to reporters after Friday prayers: the pardon does not weaken the government's resolve against corruption, and every corruption case continues according to the law. As Prime Minister, he said, his duty was to advise on the legal implications, national stability and the principle of the rule of law, but the Constitution vests the absolute power in the King. "I hope we all accept it." As for the standard operating procedure for house arrest, he said that fell under the Home Ministry.

That night, the Pakatan Harapan Presidential Council issued a statement backing the Prime Minister's position and the advice the Attorney-General and the Federal Territories Minister had given the Pardons Board. Then it added a sentence: "No one is above the law."

On the morning of the 19th, Azalina Othman Said, Umno's information chief and at the same time the Minister in the Prime Minister's Department for Law and Institutional Reform, answered back: the power of pardon is written into Article 42 of the Federal Constitution, and to describe the exercise of a constitutional power as "above the law" is inappropriate. Her closing line was almost word for word Harapan's: "No one is above the law, and no one is above the Constitution."

The same sentence, said by two sides, pointing in two directions.

MCA president Wee Ka Siong had in fact said it on the 18th already: the judicial process and the pardon process are two different things, each with its own constitutional footing.

In the afternoon, DAP's central executive committee met for about three hours. Secretary-general Anthony Loke came out to announce that he would resign as Transport Minister as the party's response; DAP's other ministers and deputy ministers would stay, "to ensure political stability". He also repeated that DAP respects the King's power of pardon.

PKR secretary-general Fadhlina Sidek said the same day that PKR stood with the Presidential Council. Ahmad Zahid Hamidi also said the same day that relations among the unity government's parties remained good.

So by Saturday evening, this is how the unity government had responded to one decision: the Prime Minister hoped everyone would accept it; Harapan said no one is above the law; Umno said the power of pardon was in the Constitution all along, and MCA said much the same; DAP had one man on his way out of office and everyone else staying.

Four responses, one cabinet.

PAS secretary-general Takiyuddin Hassan's statement on the afternoon of the 18th, as Sin Chew Daily rendered it: gratitude to the King; a development of great meaning to Najib's family; a prayer for his continued health while serving his sentence at home; and confidence that he can still contribute to the national interest through his ideas, views and experience. Four sentences, and that was that.

Umno was busy with something else. On the 18th itself the party launched a fund to help Najib pay the RM50 million. Oriental Daily's figure on the morning of the 19th: past RM1 million, with RM49 million still to go.

On the morning of the 19th, vehicles bearing the Prisons Department's markings drove into his house on Jalan Langgak Duta. Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution, speaking in Kulim, said the Prisons Department was still waiting for the Pardons Board's formal letter of notification, which usually comes from the Legal Affairs Division, and would only know its next step once it arrived. He also acknowledged that Malaysia has no specific law on house arrest, and that the ministry had earlier obtained the cabinet's agreement in principle to start drafting one, to ease prison overcrowding caused by remand detainees.

So on Saturday night, Najib was still in Kajang Prison.

"No one is above the law": Harapan said it, and so did Umno. Rania Aljunied of Lawyers for Liberty turned to the same Constitution, Article 42(4), and made a different point.

On her reading, the mechanism in that clause runs like this: the King exercises the power of pardon on the advice of the Federal Territories Pardons Board, and sitting on that board are the Federal Territories Minister, representing the cabinet, the Attorney-General, the government's legal adviser, and other members appointed on the government's advice. So when Anwar says "absolute power" and she says "exercised on the advice of the Pardons Board", the two are describing the two ends of one clause: the decision rests with the King, and the advice comes from a table the government largely fills. That, she argued, is why the government cannot say the matter has nothing to do with it.

Article 42 has always been there, and so have the seats on the Pardons Board. What is new in this day and a half is that one government has given four responses to one decision.

When a family's account book is opened, everyone in the house tells a different story. An outsider need not interrupt. Copy down what each one says, and lay the pages side by side.

There is one question everybody could answer and nobody has: what exactly do the pardon's conditions say? Rafizi Ramli has asked for all of them to be published; the Centre for Independent Journalism has asked for the legal basis, the full conditions and the monitoring arrangements to be disclosed. The statement contains a single clause: he must comply with the conditions imposed.

This does not need a constitutional amendment. Once the Pardons Board's letter is issued, the Legal Affairs Division publishes the attached conditions in full; Anwar himself has said the enforcement procedure is the Home Ministry's, so the Home Ministry sets out that procedure alongside them. It costs nothing. Whether it is done within thirty days will be plain to see.

One decision, one statement, four responses. There has been no shortage of explanations. What is missing is the one page of conditions.
