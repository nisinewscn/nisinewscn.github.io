神权国的天花板

A Theocracy With a Ceiling

逆流 · 2026年8月12日

投伊党，就是把神权国请进来。这句话，很多人心里都留着一个位置。这个星期，丹州州议会无异议通过新的伊斯兰刑事法案，那个位置好像又被点亮一次。

这项法案能通过，起点是一场败诉。二〇二四年二月九日，联邦法院以八票对一票，裁定丹州二〇一九年伊斯兰刑事法典十八项被挑战的条文中，十六项无效：理由是条文管到了国会才有权管的刑事事务，州议会越权了。

举个例子：其中一条被判无效的条文，原本处理的是伪造文件与假证供。法院说，这本质上是妨碍司法公正的普通刑事罪行，只是用宗教语言包装，管辖权还是国会的。

这星期通过的新法案，九个部分、六十二条文，补的正是这十六个空缺。

新法案能判多重？答案写在一九六五年，不是今年。伊斯兰法庭的量刑上限（监禁不超过三年、罚款不超过五千令吉、鞭刑不超过六下），是一九六五年联邦《伊斯兰法庭（刑事管辖权）法令》第二条定下的天花板，全国每一个设有伊斯兰刑事法令的州属都锁在同一个数字底下。丹州议员这次无异议通过，动不了这道天花板一分。

副州务大臣拿督莫哈末法兹里哈山说得直白：法院裁决之后，州政府成立了一个由宪法专家、伊斯兰法律专家、学者、宗教司组成的特别委员会，专责重新编排那十六项条文。按这个说法，这次立法的顺序，是法院先划线，州政府照线重画一次。

新法案实际管什么？根据星报整理的条文，涉及变性、男扮女装、暴露羞体、破坏婚姻、在伊斯兰法庭做假证供、公开或私下饮酒，条文只适用于穆斯林。刑罚按罪行轻重不同：最轻的暴露羞体，监禁不超过六个月、罚款不超过一千令吉；最重的私处相关罪行与假证供，可监禁三年、罚款五千令吉，部分罪行另加鞭刑三到六下。

这道穆斯林专属的界线，不是这次法案额外让步换来的。宪法把伊斯兰法列为归各州管的事务，同时写明伊斯兰法只适用于穆斯林，伊斯兰法庭对非穆斯林没有管辖权。这条界线，比这次立法早了几十年。

这几条离"神权国"三个字通常唤起的画面（石刑、断肢、宗教法凌驾一切公民）还隔着一层。那类刑罚不在这个天花板底下，得国会修改另一条法律；丹登两州早年通过的胡都法，至今仍因抵触这条联邦法令无法执行。

如果"神权国"指的是没有边界的宗教法，那这个星期通过的，可能不是它。边界画在一九六五年，画的人是国会。丹州议会这次全票支持的，是在边界里面把被推翻的条文补齐，边界本身没有变。

下次再听到"投伊党，神权国就来了"，可以先问一句：这次通过的法案，天花板是谁写的？

A Theocracy With a Ceiling

A vote for PAS, the saying goes, is a vote to let a theocracy in the door: many people carry that line somewhere in their head. This week, Kelantan's state assembly passed a new syariah (Islamic law) criminal bill without objection, and the line lit up again.

The bill exists because of a defeat. On 9 February 2024, the Federal Court ruled 8 to 1 that sixteen of the eighteen challenged provisions in Kelantan's 2019 Syariah Criminal Code were void. The court's finding turned on jurisdiction: the provisions reached into criminal matters that only Parliament has power over, and the state assembly had overstepped.

Take one example: one of the voided provisions dealt with forging documents and giving false evidence. The court said this was, in substance, an ordinary criminal offence about obstructing justice, dressed in religious language. The power to legislate it still belonged to Parliament.

This week's replacement bill, nine parts and sixty-two clauses, fills exactly those sixteen gaps.

How severe can the new bill's sentences get? The answer was fixed in 1965, not this year. The ceiling on what a Syariah court may impose (no more than three years' imprisonment, a fine of no more than RM5,000, or six strokes of the cane) comes from Section 2 of the federal Syariah Courts (Criminal Jurisdiction) Act 1965, and every state with a syariah criminal enactment is locked under the same figure. Kelantan's assemblymen passed this bill unanimously and still could not move that ceiling by an inch.

Deputy Menteri Besar (deputy chief minister) Dato' Mohamed Fadzli Hassan put it plainly: after the court's ruling, the state government formed a special committee of constitutional experts, Islamic-law experts, academics and religious officers to redraft the sixteen provisions. On this account, the sequence ran: the court drew the line first, and the state government redrew inside it.

What does the new bill actually cover? Per the provisions summarised by The Star, it addresses gender change, cross-dressing, exposing aurat (parts of the body Islamic law requires covered), undermining a marriage, giving false evidence before a Syariah court, and drinking alcohol in public or private, and the provisions apply only to Muslims. The penalties scale with the offence: the lightest, exposing aurat, carries up to six months' imprisonment and a fine of no more than RM1,000; the heaviest, offences involving private parts and false evidence, can bring three years' imprisonment and a RM5,000 fine, with some offences adding three to six strokes of the cane.

That Muslims-only boundary is not something conceded for this bill. The Constitution treats Islamic law as a matter for the states, and states plainly that Islamic law applies only to Muslims: Syariah courts have no jurisdiction over non-Muslims. That boundary predates this legislation by decades.

That is still some distance from the picture the word "theocracy" usually conjures: stoning, amputation, religious law overriding every citizen. Those penalties sit outside this ceiling and would require Parliament to amend a different law. The hudud (Islamic penal code, including corporal and capital punishment) enactments Kelantan and Terengganu passed years ago still cannot be enforced because they conflict with that federal law.

If "theocracy" means religious law without a boundary, then what passed this week may not be it. The boundary was drawn in 1965, by Parliament. What Kelantan's assembly voted through unanimously this week was, within that boundary, a refill of the provisions the court had struck down. The boundary itself did not move.

Next time someone says a vote for PAS lets a theocracy in the door, you can ask one thing first: who wrote the ceiling on this week's bill?
