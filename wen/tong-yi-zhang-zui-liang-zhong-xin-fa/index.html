同一张嘴，两种信法

The Same Mouth, Believed Two Ways

逆流 · 2026年7月30日

这几天你的群组里多半出现过那句话：沙努西叫华人滚回中国。截图配红字，转发语写着"看清伊党真面目"。你生气，正常。我第一眼看到，也生气。

但我想先算一笔账，再决定这口气怎么出。默迪卡民调中心在上届大选投票前做过全国民调，二〇二二年十月十九日至二十八日，一千二百零九名受访者。华裔受访者支持希盟的，四十七巴仙；支持国阵的，五巴仙；支持国盟的——一巴仙。

一巴仙。

这句话冒犯得了你，冒犯不了你的选票。你的票，本来就不在他那里。

先把两句话摆在一起看。七月二十三日晚，森美兰任抹（Jempol）的一场讲座上，伊党籍吉打州务大臣沙努西说的那句话，东方日报的译法是："华人有中国、印度人有印度，马来人只有马来土地。"转发到你手机里的版本是：叫华人回中国。第一句讲的是历史渊源，讲得对不对，可以辩；第二句是驱逐令。

两句不一样。

沙努西否认说过第二句。他给一名公正党领袖（Dr N Sathia Prakash）发了律师信，日期是七月二十七日，指对方扭曲他的话；警方也表明会向他录取口供。他没退，最新的说法还是那套：他谈的是历史，没有叫任何族群离开这个国家，还反问为什么历史不能当成事实来接受。谁对谁错，录音和法庭会给答案，轮不到我给。

有意思的是后面这一段。沙努西辩解说，他祖父从中国南来，娶了马来女子，他不可能歧视华人。彭亨行动党主席Lee Chin Chen回应，祖籍不相干，还质疑他"把非马来选民当傻瓜"。你看这套秤：他说难听话，每个字都算数，截图、报案、上头条；他解释，一个字都不作数。

再看日历。讲座在七月二十三日，森州投票在八月一日，中间隔九天。陆兆福七月二十三日刚说过，行动党"不能只关注华裔"，"必须继续坚持跨越种族的路线"，因为混合选区越来越多，单靠华人票赢不了。一场让华社同仇敌忾的怒火，烧在投票前九天，对谁最有用？这题我不答，日历替我答。

东方日报有时评人说，跟伊党合作是国阵的"票房毒药"。这话有个前提：先得有票房。一巴仙的票房，毒从何毒起？

换个算法。假如那晚沙努西什么都没说，或者只说了几句四平八稳的官话，你会投他吗？不会。假如他上台夸华社勤劳、夸中华文化五千年，你会投他吗？也不会。他说什么，你的票都不动。既然如此，这场怒火烧不到他什么。烧掉的是你的注意力，和你本来可以用来算账的九天。

你可以继续不信任沙努西。那是你的权利，也有你的道理。我只提醒一件事：他的解释，你一个字不信；那句被扭曲的话，你每个字都信。同一张嘴，两种信法。你信的到底是那张嘴，还是心里那句早就写好的台词？

The Same Mouth, Believed Two Ways

In the past few days that line has probably appeared in your group chats: Sanusi told the Chinese to go back to China. A screenshot, red captions, and a forwarding note that reads "see the true face of PAS". You were angry. That is normal. The first time I saw it, so was I.

But I want to do a sum first, before deciding how to spend that anger. Merdeka Center ran a nationwide poll before the last general election, from 19 to 28 October 2022, with 1,209 respondents. Among ethnic Chinese respondents, 47 per cent supported Pakatan Harapan; 5 per cent supported Barisan Nasional; support for Perikatan Nasional stood at one per cent.

One per cent.

The sentence can offend you. It cannot offend your vote. Your vote was never with him to begin with.

Put the two sentences side by side first. On the night of 23 July, at a ceramah (political rally talk) in Jempol, Negeri Sembilan, what Sanusi, the PAS menteri besar of Kedah, said was rendered by Oriental Daily as: "the Chinese have China, the Indians have India, the Malays have only Tanah Melayu." The version forwarded to your phone was: go back to China. The first is a claim about historical origins, arguable either way; the second is an expulsion order.

They are not the same sentence.

Sanusi denies saying the second one. He sent a letter of demand, dated 27 July, to a PKR leader (Dr N Sathia Prakash), accusing him of twisting his words; the police have said they will take his statement. He has not backed down. His latest position is the same: he was talking about history, he was not asking any community to leave this country, and he asked why history cannot be accepted as fact. Who is right will be settled by the recording and the courts, not by me.

The interesting part comes after. In his defence, Sanusi said his grandfather came south from China and married a Malay woman, so he could not possibly be prejudiced against the Chinese. Pahang DAP chief Lee Chin Chen replied that ancestry is irrelevant, and asked whether Sanusi was taking non-Malay voters for fools. Look at this set of scales: when he says something ugly, every word counts, and is screenshot, reported to the police, splashed across headlines; when he explains, not one word is admitted into evidence.

Now look at the calendar. The ceramah was on 23 July. Negeri Sembilan votes on 1 August. Nine days in between. On 23 July, Loke Siew Fook had just said that DAP "cannot focus only on the Chinese" and "must keep to the cross-ethnic path", because mixed seats are multiplying and Chinese votes (undi, votes) alone can no longer win. A wave of anger that unites the Chinese community, burning nine days before polling: who does it serve best? I will not answer that one. The calendar answers it for me.

A commentator in Oriental Daily said that working with PAS is "box-office poison" for Barisan Nasional. That claim rests on a premise: there has to be a box office first. With a box office of one per cent, what exactly is there to poison?

Try a different sum. If Sanusi had said nothing that night, or offered a few bland official lines, would you vote for him? No. If he had gone on stage to praise the diligence of the Chinese community and five thousand years of Chinese civilisation, would you vote for him? Also no. Whatever he says, your vote does not move. In which case this fire is not burning anything of his. What it burns is your attention, and the nine days you could have spent doing the sums.

You can go on distrusting Sanusi. That is your right, and you have your reasons. I would only point out one thing: when he explains, you believe not a single word; when his words are twisted, you believe every one. The same mouth, believed two ways. Is it really the mouth you believe, or the old script already written in your head?
