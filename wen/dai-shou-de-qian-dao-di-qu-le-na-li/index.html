代收的钱，到底去了哪里？

Where Did the Collected Money Go?

逆流 · 2026年10月5日

用户缴了路税，政府却未必即时收到款项。真正该追问的，不只是“欠了多少钱”，而是：这笔钱在账期内放在哪里？谁在使用它？产生的利息或资金收益，又归谁？

陆兆福在10月5日的记者会上指出，MyEG与Zetrix AI尚未转交给政府的代收款达3亿1,400万令吉，涉及5月19日至9月30日，横跨四个多月。换言之，公众已完成付款，相关交易也已发生，但政府应收的现金并未同步进入国库。

这不是单纯的行政延误，而是一个资金占用问题。

账期不是小事

可以把它想成茶餐室代收账款：顾客已经付钱给平台，平台却不是当天把钱交给店主，而是先留在自己手上，隔一段时间才结算。账期越长，平台手上暂存的资金就越大。

但路税、驾照更新费等款项并不是普通商业收入，而是应当归属政府的公共资金。公众付款后，这笔钱原则上已不属于代收代理可自由调度的资金。即使最终都会偿还，只要中间存在数周甚至数月的账期，资金价值已经发生转移。

问题因此必须问得更具体：

  • 3亿1,400万令吉在这四个多月期间，实际存放在哪些银行账户？
  • 这些账户是否产生利息、隔夜收益、现金管理收益或其他投资回报？
  • 若有收益，收益由谁取得：代收代理、关联公司、金融机构，还是政府？
  • 代收协议是否允许代理保留资金如此长时间？
  • 协议规定的结算周期究竟是每日、每周、每月，还是可以长期滚存？
  • 是否有托管账户、独立信托账户或自动划拨机制，以确保公众缴款不会与企业营运资金混在一起？
  • 政府部门是否长期容许这种“先收款、后交款”的安排？若是，谁批准、依据是什么、监督机制又在哪里？

谁承担资金成本？

若公众已付款，但政府要等待四个多月才收到钱，实际承担资金成本的，是国库，也就是纳税人。

因为政府原本可以把这笔收入用于日常行政、道路维护、执法、公共交通、医疗、教育或其他公共服务；即使暂时不使用，也可以存放在政府账户中产生收益。如今，这笔资金却在账期内滞留于代收体系。

以3亿1,400万令吉计算，即使只按相对保守的年化3%至4%资金收益率估算，四个月左右的潜在收益已不是小数目：

RM314juta×3%×124≈RM3.14juta

若按4%计算：

RM314juta×4%×124≈RM4.19juta

这只是粗略的资金时间价值估算，并不代表任何一方实际已赚取这笔收益。真正答案必须由完整银行流水、代收协议、结算时间表、托管安排及审计记录来确认。

但原则很简单：谁控制资金，谁就可能从资金的流动性、存款利息、短期投资回报或营运周转中受益。

暂停资格只是第一步

陆兆福表示，陆交局已从10月5日凌晨起暂停MyEG与Zetrix AI的代收代理资格，并表示欠款还清后才会考虑恢复资格。已经缴费更新驾照的用户则不会受到影响。

这项暂停是必要的止血措施，但公众需要的不只是“钱会不会还”，而是更完整的交代：

  1. 欠款是否已经全数追回，包括应计利息或迟延付款成本？
  2. 从5月19日至9月30日之间，每一笔公众付款的实际收款日、应交日和实际交付日分别是什么？
  3. 政府是否曾发出催款、违约通知或采取其他追讨措施？
  4. 代理是否涉及违反代收协议、信托责任、财务监管义务或其他法定要求？
  5. 若代收资金被用于企业营运、偿债、投资或关联交易，政府是否会展开独立审计或调查？
  6. 今后是否强制采用每日自动结算，杜绝数月账期重演？

MyEG自2000年起担任陆交局代收代理，陆兆福也指出，它长期是交易量与交易金额最大的代理。即使目前已有MyJPJ等直接渠道，仍有约两成用户通过代理办理相关事务。

因此，问题不只是某一次拖欠，而是制度为何允许一个涉及公共收费的代收安排，在运行26年后，仍可能形成如此大的资金滞留。

政府应直接收款

MyJPJ是政府直接面对公众的渠道。陆交局预计11月进一步增加功能，并开放予外国人使用。政策方向应当清楚：凡是路税、驾照更新、罚款及其他政府规费，最终都应尽可能通过直接、可即时结算、可实时对账的官方系统处理。

代理可以提供便利，但不能成为公共资金长期停留的“中间仓”。

真正需要建立的，是一套不能依赖任何企业自觉的制度：

  • 用户付款后，资金自动进入政府指定托管或收款账户。
  • 代理只负责交易界面、身份验证与服务流程，不持有公共款项。
  • 每日自动清算，并由政府、银行与系统记录三方对账。
  • 公众可即时取得政府正式电子收据，而非仅有平台代理付款证明。
  • 超过结算期限即自动触发罚息、暂停资格与公开披露机制。
  • 所有重大代收代理定期接受独立审计，并向国会或公众公布关键结算数据。

路税是政府收费，不是企业的浮存金（float）。公众把钱交出去的那一刻，政府就应收到；若不能即时收到，至少必须清楚说明：钱在哪里、谁在掌控、期间产生了什么收益，以及这笔收益最后进了谁的口袋。

Where Did the Collected Money Go?

Users pay their road tax, but the government does not necessarily receive the money right away. The real question is not only "how much is owed" but this: where is the money held during the settlement period? Who is using it? And who owns the interest or investment returns it generates?

At a press conference on 5 October, Loke Siew Fook said the collected payments that MyEG and Zetrix AI have not yet remitted to the government total RM314 million, covering 19 May to 30 September, a span of more than four months. In other words, the public has paid and the transactions have taken place, but the cash owed to the government has not entered the treasury at the same time.

This is not a mere administrative delay. It is a question of funds being held back.

A settlement period is no small matter

Think of a kopitiam collecting payments on someone's behalf: the customer pays the platform, but the platform does not hand the money to the shop owner that day. It keeps the money first and settles after some time. The longer the settlement period, the larger the sum the platform holds.

But road tax, licence renewal fees and the like are not ordinary commercial income. They are public funds that belong to the government. Once the public has paid, that money in principle is no longer something the collection agent can freely deploy. Even if everything is eventually repaid, as long as there is a settlement period of weeks or months in between, the value of the money has already shifted.

The questions therefore have to be put more specifically:

  • Over these four-plus months, in which bank accounts was the RM314 million actually held?
  • Did those accounts generate interest, overnight returns, cash-management yields or other investment returns?
  • If there were returns, who took them: the collection agents, related companies, financial institutions, or the government?
  • Does the collection agreement allow an agent to hold funds for so long?
  • What settlement cycle does the agreement actually stipulate: daily, weekly, monthly, or can balances roll over indefinitely?
  • Is there an escrow account, an independent trust account or an automatic transfer mechanism to ensure public payments are not mixed with a company's working capital?
  • Has the government department long tolerated this arrangement of "collect first, remit later"? If so, who approved it, on what basis, and where is the oversight?

Who bears the cost of the funds?

If the public has paid but the government must wait more than four months to receive the money, the one bearing the cost of funds is the treasury, which is to say the taxpayer.

The government could have used this income for day-to-day administration, road maintenance, enforcement, public transport, healthcare, education or other public services; even if it was not spent for the time being, it could have been placed in a government account to earn a return. Instead, the money stayed in the collection system during the settlement period.

On RM314 million, even a relatively conservative annualised yield of 3% to 4% would make the potential return over roughly four months no small amount:

RM314 million × 3% × 124 days ≈ RM3.14 million

At 4%:

RM314 million × 4% × 124 days ≈ RM4.19 million

This is only a rough estimate of the time value of money. It does not mean any party has actually earned this return. The real answer must be confirmed by full bank statements, the collection agreements, settlement schedules, custody arrangements and audit records.

But the principle is simple: whoever controls the funds may benefit from their liquidity, deposit interest, short-term investment returns or working-capital turnover.

Suspension is only the first step

Loke said JPJ has suspended MyEG's and Zetrix AI's status as collection agents from the early hours of 5 October, and that reinstatement will be considered only after the debt is cleared. Users who have already paid to renew their licences will not be affected.

The suspension is a necessary measure to stop the bleeding, but the public needs more than "will the money be repaid". It needs a fuller account:

  1. Has the debt been fully recovered, including accrued interest or the cost of late payment?
  2. For every public payment between 19 May and 30 September, what were the actual date of collection, the due date for remittance and the actual date of remittance?
  3. Did the government issue reminders or default notices, or take other recovery action?
  4. Is the agent involved in any breach of the collection agreement, trust duties, financial regulatory obligations or other statutory requirements?
  5. If the collected funds were used for business operations, debt repayment, investments or related-party transactions, will the government open an independent audit or investigation?
  6. Going forward, will daily automatic settlement be made mandatory, so that a months-long settlement period never happens again?

MyEG has been a JPJ collection agent since 2000, and Loke also noted that it has long been the agent with the largest transaction volume and value. Even though direct channels such as MyJPJ now exist, about 20 per cent of users still go through agents.

So the problem is not just a single instance of late remittance. It is why the system allowed a collection arrangement involving public fees to still build up such a large retention of funds after 26 years in operation.

The government should collect directly

MyJPJ is the channel through which the government deals with the public directly. JPJ expects to add more functions in November and open it to foreigners. The policy direction should be clear: road tax, licence renewals, fines and other government fees should ultimately be handled, as far as possible, through official systems that settle immediately and reconcile in real time.

Agents can offer convenience, but they must not become a "middle warehouse" where public money sits for long periods.

What needs to be built is a system that does not depend on the goodwill of any company:

  • After the user pays, the funds go automatically into a government-designated escrow or collection account.
  • Agents handle only the transaction interface, identity verification and service process, and do not hold public money.
  • Settlement runs automatically every day, with three-way reconciliation between the government, the bank and the system records.
  • The public can immediately obtain an official government electronic receipt, not just a platform's proof of agent payment.
  • Passing the settlement deadline automatically triggers penalty interest, suspension of status and public disclosure.
  • All major collection agents undergo regular independent audits and publish key settlement data to Parliament or the public.

Road tax is a government charge, not a company's float. The moment the public hands over the money, the government should have it; if it cannot be received immediately, it must at least be made clear where the money is, who controls it, what returns it generated in the meantime, and whose pocket those returns ended up in.
