三年，两个森美兰

Three Years, Two Negeri Sembilans

逆流 · 2026年8月3日

安华七月二十二日在森美兰的一场助选活动上说，团结政府在中央层面稳定而牢固，部长之间合作顺畅，地方怎么选是地方的事，中央这边没有问题。这句话，你这几年应该听过不止一次：无论地方闹得多凶，团结政府始终是稳的。

十天后，森美兰给了不一样的答案。

八月一日，选委会公布的成绩：国阵十八席、希盟十一席、国盟七席，三十六席全数出炉。三年前，二〇二三年那一届，同一个森美兰交出的是另一张成绩单：希盟十七席加国阵十四席，两党合共三十一席，一起组了州政府；国盟五席，坐在对面。三年后，位置换了：国阵十八席加国盟七席，二十五席合组新政府；希盟十一席，这一次坐到了对面。

行动党秘书长陆兆福守了三届的真纳州议席，也在这一夜，从政二十二年来第一次落选，输给马华新人萧康骏。

这不是森美兰单独的故事。往前数三场州选：二〇二五年十一月二十九日的沙巴州选，希盟七十三席里只保住一席，行动党四席全部落空，得票率六点七三巴仙；二〇二六年七月十一日的柔佛州选，国阵五十六席里拿下四十八席，希盟八席，国盟一席都没拿到；八月一日轮到森美兰。三场州选，三个不同的州，隔着不到一年，希盟没有一次拿下州政府。

这不是反对党替希盟写的注脚。投票当天，希盟自己发的联合文告承认，这一届森美兰州选"充斥著前所未有且几乎完全围绕身份认同的种族政治宣传"，足以"播下分裂社会和森州人民团结的种子"。希盟森州选举竞选主任阿米鲁丁也公开说，身份政治和种族议题成了这次选举的主要因素，经济、就业、教育、生活成本这些民生课题，反而被挤到了后头。

如果团结说的是内阁名单有没有换人，安华没有说错，团结政府确实没变。但如果团结说的是这两个政党在选民面前站不站在同一边，森美兰三年内给出的，是两张相反的成绩单。国阵这一次没有跟希盟一起守住森美兰，换成跟国盟一起把它拿下。中央那张合照没换人，地方这盘棋，走的方向不一样了。

行动党的全国代表大会原定七月十二日举行，因柔佛和森美兰州议会相继解散而改期到八月十六日。改期后的这一场大会，其中一项议程，就是党内的部长和副部长要不要交出内阁职位。这不是我猜的，是他们自己排定的议程。

下次再听到"团结政府很稳"，你可以先问一句：稳的是内阁的位子，还是这些政党站的位置？答案不在文告里，在选委会的开票记录里。

Three Years, Two Negeri Sembilans

On 22 July, at a campaign event in Negeri Sembilan, Anwar Ibrahim said the unity government remained stable and firm at the federal level, that cooperation among ministers was smooth, that whatever happened in a state election was a state matter, and that there was no problem at the centre. You have probably heard some version of that more than once these past few years: however messy things get at state level, the unity government stays stable.

Ten days later, Negeri Sembilan gave a different answer.

On 1 August, the Election Commission's results were in: Barisan Nasional (BN) 18 seats, Pakatan Harapan (PH) 11 seats, Perikatan Nasional (PN) 7 seats, all 36 seats declared. Three years earlier, in the 2023 election, the same state had produced a different scorecard: PH's 17 seats plus BN's 14 made 31 between them, enough to form the state government together, with PN's 5 seats on the other side. Three years on, the sides had swapped: BN's 18 seats plus PN's 7 made 25, forming the new government, while PH's 11 sat on the other side this time.

DAP secretary-general Anthony Loke, who had held the Chennah seat for three consecutive terms, lost that night for the first time in 22 years in politics, beaten by MCA newcomer Siow Kong Choon.

This is not a story unique to Negeri Sembilan. Look back at three state elections: in the Sabah state election on 29 November 2025, PH held on to just 1 of 73 seats, DAP lost all 4 of its own, on a vote share of 6.73 per cent; in the Johor state election on 11 July 2026, BN took 48 of 56 seats, PH took 8, and PN took none at all; then, on 1 August, it was Negeri Sembilan's turn. Three state elections, three different states, in under a year, and PH did not win control of the state government in any of them.

This is not the opposition's footnote to PH's own record. On polling day, PH's own joint statement admitted that this Negeri Sembilan election was "filled with unprecedented racial political propaganda almost entirely centred on identity politics," enough to "sow the seeds of dividing society and Negeri Sembilan's unity." Amirudin, PH's campaign director for the Negeri Sembilan election, said publicly that identity politics and racial issues had become the main factor in this election, crowding out livelihood issues such as the economy, jobs, education and the cost of living.

If unity means whether the cabinet list has changed, Anwar was not wrong: the unity government really has not changed. But if unity means whether these two parties stand on the same side in front of voters, Negeri Sembilan has produced two opposite scorecards within three years. This time, BN did not join PH to hold Negeri Sembilan; it joined PN to take it instead. The photograph of the cabinet at the centre still has the same faces in it. The game being played at state level has moved in a different direction.

DAP's national congress was originally due on 12 July but was postponed to 16 August after the Johor and Negeri Sembilan state assemblies were dissolved one after another. One item on the rescheduled congress's agenda is whether the party's ministers and deputy ministers should give up their cabinet posts. That is not my guess. It is the agenda they set themselves.

Next time someone tells you the unity government is stable, you can ask first: stable in the cabinet seats, or stable in where these parties stand? The answer is not in a press statement. It is in the Election Commission's vote count.
