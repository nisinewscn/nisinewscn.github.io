穷不等于账乱

Poor Is Not the Same as Sloppy Books

逆流 · 2026年8月5日

伊党执政的州，穷，这几乎是共识。丹州、吉打、登嘉楼，念叨了几十年，宗教治理办不好经济，账目大概也好不到哪里去。七月尾，英文网络媒体FMT一篇评论这样写：伊党执政的三个州，比"较富裕的州属"贫穷率更高；槟城这类由行动党长年执政的州，经济表现明显更强。

评论没有附数字。

先把贫穷率摆出来，不必回避。统计局最新一份州属贫穷发生率，2024年数字：丹州11.5巴仙，吉打8.4巴仙，登嘉楼5.8巴仙。同一份表里，槟城1.9巴仙，雪兰莪不到1巴仙。这个差距是真的。

穷不等于账乱，这才是要查的地方。

这跟说"某个人赚得少，所以他一定不会理财"，是同一种推理。收入的数字，跟管钱的功夫，本来就是两件事，家里记账的人都知道。

"账目办得好不好"，贫穷发生率这张表回答不了，它量的是家庭收入，不是政府账本。真正查账本的，是国家审计总长，每年把州政府财政报表的审计意见交给国会。

2025年10月呈上的那一份，审的是2024年度州政府账目：登嘉楼拿到"无保留意见"，跟槟城同一个等级。吉打、丹州、雪兰莪，跟另外六个州一起，拿到的是"无保留意见，附加其他事项段"。往前一年，2024年10月那一份审的是2023年度：登嘉楼、吉打、丹州、槟城、雪兰莪，五个州全部落在同一个等级，跟森美兰、马六甲、玻璃市挤在一起；那一年拿到最干净等级的，是柔佛、彭亨、霹雳、砂拉越、沙巴。

"附加其他事项段"本身仍然属于"无保留意见"，跟"保留意见"是两回事：审计总长用的官方名称就是"无保留意见，附加其他事项段"，不是"保留意见"。那一份2023年度的报表，审计总长另外提出两百六十项建议，交给各州相关机构参考。两年的名单摆在一起看，十三个州里，只有彭亨两年都没被点名附加其他事项段；其余十二个州，登嘉楼、槟城、雪兰莪都在内，都至少上过一次名单，跟哪个党执政没有固定关系。

贫穷率和审计意见，量的是两件不同的事。前者记的是几十年累积下来的经济起点，不是哪一届州政府一年做出来的。后者记的是当下管账的功夫。

登嘉楼从"附加其他事项段"，做到跟槟城同一格，用了一年。至于具体是哪一项做对了，两份报告都没有细说，这里也不猜。账目这件事，是可以改的，不像"宗教治理注定办不好经济"这种说法，听起来像是写死的。如果把"穷"直接读成"账乱"，量错了尺，答案自然跟着错。

下次再看到"宗教治理，账目一塌糊涂"这类说法，不妨翻开审计总长报告，找到自己在意的那个州，看看它跟哪些州分在同一格。答案不在评论文章里，在报表里。

Poor Is Not the Same as Sloppy Books

It is close to a consensus that PAS-governed states are poor. Kelantan, Kedah and Terengganu have carried the label for decades: religious governance cannot manage an economy, so the accounts are probably no better. In late July, an FMT (Free Malaysia Today) commentary put it plainly: the three PAS-governed states face higher poverty rates than "wealthier states", while Penang, long governed by DAP, has performed markedly better economically.

The commentary did not attach any figures.

Start with the poverty numbers, without dodging them. The Department of Statistics Malaysia's (DOSM) latest state poverty incidence figures, for 2024, show Kelantan at 11.5 per cent, Kedah at 8.4 per cent and Terengganu at 5.8 per cent. In the same table, Penang stands at 1.9 per cent and Selangor under 1 per cent. That gap is real.

Poor is not the same as sloppy accounts. That is the part worth checking.

It is the same reasoning as saying "that person earns little, so he must be bad with money." The size of an income and the skill of managing it are two different things, as anyone who keeps a household ledger knows.

Whether the accounts are well kept is not something a poverty table can answer. It measures household income, not government ledgers. The office that actually audits the ledgers is the Auditor-General, who submits an opinion on every state government's financial statements to Parliament each year.

The report tabled in October 2025 audited the 2024 accounts: Terengganu received an "unmodified opinion", the same grade as Penang. Kedah, Kelantan and Selangor, together with six other states, received an "unmodified opinion with an other matters paragraph". A year earlier, the report tabled in October 2024 audited the 2023 accounts: Terengganu, Kedah, Kelantan, Penang and Selangor, all five, landed in the same grade, alongside Negeri Sembilan, Melaka and Perlis. That year, the cleanest grade went to Johor, Pahang, Perak, Sarawak and Sabah.

An "other matters paragraph" still belongs to the "unmodified opinion" category, a different thing from a "qualified opinion": the official name the Auditor-General uses is "unmodified opinion with an other matters paragraph", not "qualified opinion". That 2023 report also came with 260 recommendations for the states concerned. Set the two years side by side: of 13 states, only Pahang was never flagged with an other matters paragraph in either year. The other twelve, Terengganu, Penang and Selangor among them, each appeared on the list at least once, with no fixed relationship to which party governs.

Poverty rates and audit opinions measure two different things. The first records decades of accumulated economic starting points, not something one state government produces in a single term. The second records how well the books are kept right now.

Terengganu went from an "other matters" flag to the same grade as Penang in a single year. Neither report spells out exactly which fix made the difference, and this piece will not guess. Bookkeeping can change; a claim that religious governance is destined to run a bad economy sounds like it never can. Read "poor" straight into "sloppy accounts", and the measurement is off, so the conclusion follows it off course.

Next time someone says religious governance means the accounts are a mess, it is worth opening the Auditor-General's report and finding the state in question, to see which states share its grade. The answer is not in the commentary. It is in the report.
