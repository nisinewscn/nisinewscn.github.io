怕，要怕得有凭据

If You Must Fear, Fear the Record

逆流 · 2026年7月29日

星期六，森州投票。这几天你大概又听到那句熟悉的话：绿潮来了，华人的日子就到头了。上个星期，沙努西在森州任期讲座上说「印度人有印度，华人有中国，马来人只有这片土地」，截图传遍了各个群组。他两天后辩解，说非巫裔作为公民的权利不受影响。恐惧，又一次准时报到。

先看一个日期。「绿潮」这个词，是二〇二二年大选之后才传开的。那一年伊党的国会议席从十八席跳到四十三席，成为最大单一政党，评论人借它的党旗颜色造了这个词。

但伊党执政吉兰丹，从一九九〇年开始。三十五年。

换句话说，如果「伊党执政，华人完蛋」是一个可以验证的说法，最完整的实验数据不在任何人的口中，在吉兰丹。账本摊在那里，比剧本早了三十一年。

翻开账本，先记实的一栏。哥打峇鲁自一九九〇年至今没有商业电影院，州政府说从未禁止，只是条件严格：影厅不可全黑，祈祷时间要停映。

万字票执照，吉兰丹一九九〇年起不发，登嘉楼二〇二〇年跟进，玻璃市最后一家投注站二〇二四年关门。吉打用不续牌的方式做同一件事，去年十二月被上诉庭裁定违宪，因为博彩的立法权在联邦，不在州。男女理发店必须分开经营，登嘉楼州行政议员说明过：华人业者，一样要遵守。这些是真的，有档案，有罚款数额，翻得到。

但同一栏账，可以有另一种读法。别的州视为理所当然的东西，这几个州偏偏不给。换一个身份再读一遍：如果你是家长，孩子要参加全国性的营队、集训、比赛，你会挑哪个州办？一个没有夜店、没有投注站、酒精有管制的地方，孩子能学坏的选项少得可怜。最坏的情况，是吃了又吃，吃完再吃。

再记另一栏。三十五年里，没有一宗经得起查证的记录，是丹州政府拆除华人庙宇，或拒绝华小的地和钱。二〇二〇年吉打拆的是两座兴都庙，州政府说那是宪报保留地上的执法，反对党和印裔团体至今不接受这个解释，你可以自己衡量这笔账。但「伊党拆华人庙」的版本，查无实据。

吉打州预算至今保留每年三十万令吉给华小和淡小。丹州独中拿不到制度化的州拨款，这是房政部长倪可敏今年五月公开点名的，也记在这栏。

至于伊斯兰刑事法：吉兰丹一九九三年全票通过，登嘉楼二〇〇二年通过，三十多年来一天都没有执行过。原因不在州，在联邦宪法：刑事权划给国会，而国会经历国阵、希盟、国盟、团结政府，谁都没有放行。

还有第三栏，最少人引用，数字却最硬。统计局的数据：吉兰丹华人从二〇一〇年的五万二千四百人，减少到二〇二五年的四万四千一百人，比例从百分之三点三跌到百分之二点三。

同一个吉兰丹，家庭收入全国最低，二〇二四年中位数四千零八十三令吉，全国是七千零一十七。贫穷率百分之十一点五，全国是百分之五点一。自来水覆盖率百分之七十三点九，全国垫底，这是总审计司报告的数字。

人为什么走？统计局不解释原因。但一个地方留不住年轻人，通常不是因为害怕，是因为薪水。

账本读完，结论不是「不用怕」三个字。结论是：该怕的对象，换了。

剧本让你怕一场突如其来的灾难：庙被拆、学校被关、生活一夜变天。账本给你的是另一种东西：一排你可以自己决定是麻烦还是安心的生活规定，一个连续多年排在最后的州经济，还有政治人物白纸黑字、可以原句引用的话，就像本文开头那一句。

如果要怕，这些才是有凭据的怕法。

星期六，你可以带着一个四年前发明的词走进投票站，也可以带着一份三十五年的账本。哪一个更可靠，你自己决定。

If You Must Fear, Fear the Record

This Saturday, Negeri Sembilan votes. These past few days you have probably heard the familiar line again: the green wave is coming, and that is the end of Chinese life here. Last week, at a rally in Negeri Sembilan, Sanusi said "Indians have India, the Chinese have China, the Malays have only this land" — the screenshot went around every group chat. Two days later he clarified that non-Malays' rights as citizens are unaffected. Fear, once again, arrived on schedule.

Start with a date. The term "green wave" only spread after the 2022 general election. That year PAS jumped from eighteen parliamentary seats to forty-three, becoming the largest single party, and commentators coined the term from the colour of its flag.

But PAS has governed Kelantan since 1990. Thirty-five years.

In other words, if "PAS rule means the end of the Chinese" is a testable claim, the most complete experimental data is not in anyone's mouth. It is in Kelantan. The ledger is lying open there, thirty-one years older than the script.

Open the ledger and record the real column first. Kota Bharu has had no commercial cinema since 1990; the state government says it never banned them, only that the conditions are strict: halls must not be fully dark, screenings pause for prayer times.

4D licences: Kelantan stopped issuing them in 1990, Terengganu followed in 2020, and the last betting outlet in Perlis closed in 2024. Kedah did the same thing by refusing licence renewals, which the Court of Appeal ruled unconstitutional last December, because the power to legislate on gambling is federal, not state. Hair salons must be gender-segregated, and a Terengganu state exco member has spelled it out: Chinese operators get no exemption. These things are real — there are files, there are fine amounts, and you can look them up.

But the same column can be read another way. What other states take for granted, these states withhold. Read it again from a different seat: if you are a parent, and your child is off to a national camp, a training stint, a tournament — which state would you pick to host it? In a place with no nightclubs, no betting outlets, and alcohol under control, the options for a teenager to go astray are pitifully few. The worst case is eating, then eating, then eating again.

Now record the other column. In thirty-five years there is not one verifiable record of the Kelantan state government demolishing a Chinese temple or refusing land and money for a Chinese primary school. What Kedah demolished in 2020 were two Hindu temples; the state said it was enforcement on gazetted reserve land, and the opposition and Indian community groups reject that explanation to this day — weigh that entry yourself. But the "PAS demolishes Chinese temples" version has no verified case behind it.

Kedah's state budget still keeps RM300,000 a year for Chinese and Tamil primary schools. Kelantan's independent Chinese secondary school gets no institutionalised state funding — Housing Minister Nga Kor Ming named this publicly in May, and it goes in this column too.

As for hudud: Kelantan passed its enactment unanimously in 1993, Terengganu in 2002, and in more than thirty years it has not been enforced for a single day. The reason lies not with the states but with the Federal Constitution: criminal law belongs to Parliament, and Parliament — under Barisan Nasional, Pakatan Harapan, Perikatan Nasional and the unity government — has never opened that gate.

There is a third column, the least quoted and the hardest-numbered. Statistics Department data: Kelantan's Chinese population fell from 52,400 in 2010 to 44,100 in 2025, its share dropping from 3.3 per cent to 2.3.

The same Kelantan has the lowest household income in the country: a 2024 median of RM4,083 against a national RM7,017. Its poverty rate is 11.5 per cent against a national 5.1. Its treated-water coverage is 73.9 per cent, the lowest of any state — a figure from the Auditor-General's report.

Why do people leave? The Statistics Department gives no reasons. But when a place cannot keep its young people, it is usually not because of fear. It is because of pay.

Read the whole ledger and the conclusion is not "nothing to fear". The conclusion is: the thing worth fearing has changed.

The script tells you to fear a sudden catastrophe: temples torn down, schools shut, life upended overnight. The ledger hands you something else: a row of lifestyle rules you can decide for yourself to call a nuisance or a comfort, a state economy that has ranked last for years on end, and words from politicians in black and white that you can quote in the original — like the one this article opened with.

If you must fear, these are the fears with evidence behind them.

On Saturday, you can walk into the polling station carrying a word invented four years ago, or carrying a thirty-five-year ledger. Which one is more reliable — decide for yourself.
