有胆量，就退出政府

If You Dare, Quit the Government

逆流 · 2026年9月16日

团结政府成立四年，最常被人挂在嘴边的一个词，是"稳"。

这个星期，"稳"这个字被自己人拆了三次。

九月十二日，巫统大会闭幕礼上，主席阿末扎希撂下一句话："团结政府之所以能够成立，是因为巫统。首相之所以能够成为首相，也是因为巫统的支持。"接着一句挑战："不要叫我们退出政府。他们挑战我们，如果有胆量就退出政府。"意思很直接：要嘛别再逼巫统，要嘛正式把巫统踢出去，重新大选。

两天后，九月十四日，行动党秘书长陆兆福在沙亚南见记者，宣布会请安华一回国就召开希盟主席理事会会议。他说，阿末扎希那番话，已经让外界担心起国家政治的确定性和稳定性，也可能影响投资人与公众的信心，首相有必要向所有成员党说清楚。这话，出自团结政府里最大华基政党秘书长之口，不是反对党嘴里的批评。

同一天，安华人在印度，把这一切形容成家里难免有的小磨擦：巫统内部的强硬声音他都听到了，但那就跟一般家庭偶尔拌嘴一样，还在控制之中。他说自己选的是"比较难走的路"："政府必须稳，不然国家会有很大损失"。

再过一天，九月十五日，林冠英公开喊话，要槟城首席部长曹观友把巫统请出槟州政府，说巫统"投机、没有原则、虚伪"。曹观友的回应：那只是林冠英的个人看法，不是党的立场，槟州跟巫统的关系怎么走，得等希盟最高领导层拍板。那场会议，刚好就是陆兆福当天已经要求安华召开的那一场。

一个星期，三次表态，说法各不相同。

团结政府从二〇二二年组成那天起，靠的从来不是理念相近，是议席数字拼出来的多数。巫统跟希盟斗了几十年，这次是先有票，后谈感情。这种联盟会不会吵架不重要，重要的是每次吵架都得回到算术：谁走了，多数还在不在。阿末扎希那句"有胆就把我们踢出去"，问的正是这道题。

执政阵营可以说这只是政治正常的拉扯，看衰的一方则爱说团结政府快散了。这星期的记录，两边都对不上号。

没人走。但也没人真的放心。

陆兆福那天的说法，是政治确定性和投资人信心可能受冲击，不是天下太平。政府散没散，这星期都不是重点。重点是，维持"没有人走"这四个字，这星期得靠一场专门开的会议才稳得住。这场会议，本来不该存在。

陆兆福已经开了口，要在安华回国后召开希盟主席理事会会议。这场会不必关起门解决完就算数：理事会可以在会后发一份简短的公开声明，把党的立场讲清楚，不用再让读者从林冠英和曹观友各自见记者的说法里，自己拼凑答案。不花一分钱。安华从马尔代夫回国后三十天内做不做得到，一查就知道。

陆兆福那句话，本来是说给记者听的。拿去问问自己：一个真正稳的联盟，需要靠一场临时会议才安得下心吗？

If You Dare, Quit the Government

Four years into Malaysia's unity government (kerajaan perpaduan, the Pakatan Harapan-Umno-led coalition formed after the 2022 election), the word attached to it most often is "stable".

This week, its own members took that word apart three times.

On 12 September, at the closing of Umno's general assembly (Perhimpunan Agung Umno, the party's annual congress), Umno president Ahmad Zahid Hamidi said: "The unity government exists because of Umno. The Prime Minister became Prime Minister because of Umno's support." Then came the dare: "Don't tell us to leave. If they have the nerve, let them kick Umno out." The meaning was plain: either stop pressuring Umno, or formally eject it and call a fresh election.

Two days later, on 14 September, DAP secretary-general Loke Siew Fook told reporters in Shah Alam that he would ask Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim to convene a Pakatan Harapan Presidential Council meeting as soon as Anwar returned home. His reason: Zahid's remarks had already raised outside worry about the country's political certainty and stability, and could affect investor and public confidence, so the Prime Minister needed to spell things out to every component party. That statement came from the secretary-general of the unity government's largest Chinese-based party, not from the opposition.

The same day, Anwar was in India, describing the whole affair as the ordinary friction no household escapes: he had heard Umno's hardliners, but it was no different from an ordinary family's occasional bickering, still under control. He said he had chosen "the harder path": "The government must be stable, or the country will suffer heavy losses."

A day after that, on 15 September, Lim Guan Eng publicly called on Penang Chief Minister Chow Kon Yeow to show Umno the door in the state government, describing Umno's leadership as "opportunistic, unprincipled and hypocritical". Chow's reply: that was Lim's personal opinion, not the party's position; Penang's relationship with Umno would be decided by Pakatan Harapan's top leadership. That meeting, as it happens, is the very one Loke had already asked Anwar to call that same day.

One week, three statements, three different versions.

The unity government was never built on shared conviction. It was built, from the day it formed in 2022, on a majority assembled out of seat counts: Umno and Harapan (Pakatan Harapan, the Anwar-led coalition) had spent decades as rivals, and this time the numbers came before any goodwill. Whether such a coalition quarrels is not the point. Every quarrel comes back to the same arithmetic: who has left, and whether the majority still holds. Zahid's dare, that they eject Umno if they have the nerve, was asking exactly that question.

The government's own camp can call this ordinary political jostling. Those betting against the coalition like to call it finished. This week's record matches neither story.

Nobody left. But nobody was at ease either.

What Loke described that day was political certainty and investor confidence coming under strain, not business as usual. Whether the government survives was never the real question this week. The real question is that keeping the words "nobody left" true took a specially convened meeting. That meeting should not have needed to happen.

Loke has already asked for a Pakatan Harapan Presidential Council meeting once Anwar is back. That meeting does not have to end behind closed doors. The Council could issue one short public statement afterwards, spelling out the party's position, so readers no longer have to piece together the answer from Lim Guan Eng and Chow Kon Yeow's separate press conferences. It costs nothing. Whether it happens within thirty days of Anwar's return from the Maldives leg of his trip is a fact anyone can check.

Loke said that to reporters at a press conference. Ask yourself this instead: does a coalition that is genuinely stable need an emergency meeting just to feel calm?
